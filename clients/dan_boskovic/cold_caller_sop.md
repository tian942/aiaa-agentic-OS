## 2. Your Mindset

Cold calling is not about selling. It's about uncovering pain and getting agreement to have a deeper conversation.

You are not trying to close someone on the phone. You are trying to book a 15-minute health check. That's it. Your job ends when they say yes.

**The Rules of a Great Cold Caller:**

1. **You are curious, not pushy.** You genuinely want to make sure their setup still holds up. When you're curious, they open up. When you're pushy, they hang up.
2. **Rejection is irrelevant.** Every "no" is just math. Don't react emotionally to it. It means nothing.
3. **You represent a real solution.** These aren't strangers — they already trust Dan. You're following up on that relationship, not cold-pitching a stranger.
4. **You don't give a fuck.** They don't know who you are beyond "I work with Dan." If you freeze — fuck it and move on.

## 4. Objection Handling Library

*(Backup/general-purpose — the script doc's own objection branches are campaign-specific and come first. Use these if something comes up that the script doesn't cover.)*

**Early Objections (Before You Even Get Started)**

- **"I'm not interested."** → *"Totally fair — most people don't think about it until something's already gone wrong. This is really just Dan making sure nothing's slipped since you two last worked together."* (If they engage even slightly, you're back in.)
- **"Send me some information."** → *"Happy to, but honestly a lot of this — backups, email security — doesn't really show up on paper. Fifteen minutes on-site beats a PDF for catching the real gaps."*
- **"We've already got someone for that."** → *"Good to hear — this isn't about replacing anyone, just a second set of eyes from someone who already knows your setup. Are you confident nothing's been missed since Dan last touched it?"*
- **"I don't have time right now."** → *"Completely get it. It's only fifteen minutes and it's on-site whenever works for you — what does your week look like?"*
- **"How did you get my number?"** → *"You're actually in Dan's own client/contact list — he wanted to personally check back in rather than let this go unfollowed."*

**Mid-Call Objections**

- **"I need to think about it."** → *"Of course — what specifically? Timing, or just whether it's worth fifteen minutes right now?"*
- **"What's changed since we last talked to Dan?"** → *"Mostly just time — setups that were solid a year or two ago quietly drift as the threat landscape moves. That's exactly what this check is for."*

## 9. Tone & Delivery Rules

Script words matter 20%. Tone matters 80%.

1. **Sound confused, not sharp.** *Wrong:* "Hi this is James calling from Alverium Cyber with a solution for your cybersecurity needs—" *Right:* "Hey, is this [Name]? Oh hey — would I completely destroy your day if I told you this was a cold call?"
2. **Slow down after the opener.** Silence is your friend. Ask, then stop talking.
3. **Drop your energy slightly when they push back.** Don't match resistance with energy.
4. **Lead with the relationship, not the pitch.** This list already knows Dan — remind them of that early and often ("he's the one who originally worked with you"), it's your strongest asset on this campaign specifically.
5. **Mirror their energy in discovery.** Detailed and analytical → match precision. Casual → be more relaxed.
6. **Never sound like you need this call.** No attachment to the outcome.

## 10. Common Mistakes to Never Make

- ❌ **Pitching before the offer step.** Follow the script's own sequence — Opener → Dream Customer → Pattern Interrupt → Scapegoat → New Belief → Mechanism → Position → Offer. Don't skip ahead.
- ❌ **Talking too much.** A call where they spoke 70% of the time is a good call.
- ❌ **Inventing a guarantee.** Alverium Cyber doesn't have one — use the 26-year track record and the Hero Case Study instead.
- ❌ **Quoting pricing tiers on this call.** This call books a free visit, it doesn't sell a tier. Redirect any pricing question to the visit itself.
- ❌ **Arguing with objections.** Validate, then redirect.
- ❌ **Not doing the end-of-day report.** You can't improve what you don't track.
- ❌ **Logging calls vaguely.** *Bad:* "No answer x5." *Good:* "Called Wed/Thur/Fri, left voicemail each time. Owner's name is Marcus, retail client since 2022. Try again Monday 8:30 AM."

## 11. FAQ for New Callers

**Q: What if I don't know the answer to something they ask about their setup?**
A: *"That's exactly what the fifteen-minute check is for — Dan will go through that with you directly rather than me guessing over the phone."* Never bluff. Redirect to the booking.

**Q: What if they ask how I got their number?**
A: Be honest — they're in Dan's own client or contact list, this is a personal follow-up, not a purchased list.

**Q: How many times should I try a number before giving up?**
A: 7-8 attempts over 7 days across different times of day. After that, mark as dead and move on.

**Q: What if someone is really rude or aggressive?**
A: Stay calm. *"No worries at all, I appreciate your time."* Hang up. Log it and move on.

**Q: What if they book a visit and then no-show?**
A: Text within 10 minutes: *"Hey [Name] — looks like we missed each other. Happy to reschedule if this week still works?"* One follow-up only. If no reply, mark as dead.

**Q: A prospect asks if Alverium Cyber offers a guarantee.**
A: *"We don't do guarantees on the security side — what we've got instead is 26 years and clients who've stayed with Dan for years, not one-off jobs."* Never improvise one.

**Q: Can I adjust the script?**
A: The structure is fixed. Once you've done 500+ calls and know the framework cold, wording can flex. Until then, stick to it exactly.
