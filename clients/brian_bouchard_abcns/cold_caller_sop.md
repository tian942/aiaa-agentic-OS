## 2. Your Mindset

Cold calling is not about selling. It's about uncovering pain and getting agreement to have a deeper conversation.

You are not trying to close someone on the phone. You are trying to book a 15-minute chat. That's it. Your job ends when they say yes to a meeting.

**The Rules of a Great Cold Caller:**

1. **You are curious, not pushy.** You genuinely want to understand their business. When you're curious, they open up. When you're pushy, they hang up.
2. **Rejection is irrelevant.** Every "no" you hear is just math. If 1 in every 100 calls books, then each "no" is worth something — it gets you closer to the "yes." Don't react emotionally to rejection. It means nothing.
3. **You represent a real solution.** The businesses we call have real problems with their IT and compliance exposure. We actually fix those problems. You're not a pest. You're someone with an answer to a problem they've already been losing sleep over.
4. **You don't give a fuck.** They don't know who you are, they don't care what you do and why you are there. If you freeze — fuck it and move on.

## 4. Objection Handling Library

**Early Objections (Before You Even Get Started)**

- **"I'm not interested."** → *"Of course you're not interested. Most people in your position have been burned by IT vendors who promise the world and deliver absolutely nothing. However, what we do is different."* (If they engage even slightly, you're back in.)
- **"Send me some information."** → *"Happy to, but when was sending some information better than a genuine 3 minute talk??"*
- **"We've already got someone for that."** → *"That's actually perfect, so you're already invested in this area. I'm not here to replace anyone. The question I'd be curious about is, are you happy with the results you're getting right now?"*
- **"I don't have time right now."** → *"Completely get it. The call itself is only 15 minutes, just let me know which day and time works best for you and I'll get you booked in?"*
- **"How did you get my number?"** → *"Honestly, I just have a list of the most amazing [medical practices / AEC firms] in the area. Happy to explain more, but first, it seems like you're pretty busy already right?"*

**Mid-Call Objections**

- **"I've tried IT vendors before and it didn't work."** → *"Yeah most of them are shit anyways, that's why we operate on a partnership level with a money-back guarantee. What was the biggest problem with your last provider?"*
- **"What makes you different from other IT companies?"** → *"Honestly, the fact that if you're not happy within 60 days, we write you a $500 check — no long contract, no handcuffs. We're not trying to lock you in, we're trying to earn it."*
- **"I need to think about it."** → *"Of course, what specifically do you need to think through? Is it timing, the current provider situation, or something about the process?"*
- **"We don't have the budget right now."** → *"Totally understand. Can I ask, when you say budget, is it a cash flow thing right now, or more that you're not sure it's worth the spend yet compared to what you're already paying?"*

## 9. Tone & Delivery Rules

Script words matter 20%. Tone matters 80%.

1. **Sound confused, not sharp.** Sounding sharp and professional immediately signals "salesperson" — their guard goes up. Sound slightly unsure, like you're genuinely working something out. *Wrong:* "Hi this is James calling from ABCNS with a solution for your IT challenges—" *Right:* "Hi, is this [Name]? Hey — would I completely destroy your day if I told you this is a cold call?"
2. **Slow down after the opener.** Most callers rush, scared of silence. Silence is your friend — it creates space for the prospect to respond. Ask, then stop talking. Wait.
3. **Drop your energy slightly when they push back.** Don't match resistance with energy. When they say "I'm not interested," don't perk up and pitch harder — lower your energy, slow your speech, sound like you genuinely accept what they said, then ask the one question that might open them back up.
4. **Smile when you deliver a light, playful line.** You can hear a smile on the phone. Playful, light — not defensive or salesy.
5. **Mirror their energy in the discovery phase.** Detailed and analytical → match precision. Casual and chatty → be more relaxed. Busy and direct → get to the point faster.
6. **Never sound like you need this call.** As soon as desperation enters your voice, the call dies. You are one of many options; they are one of many leads. No attachment to the outcome.

## 10. Common Mistakes to Never Make

- ❌ **Pitching before you've found pain.** The only time you pitch is Step 7, after they've told you their pain, you've reflected it back, and they've agreed. Before that — questions only.
- ❌ **Talking too much.** A call where they spoke 70% of the time is a good call. A call where you spoke 70% of the time is a bad call.
- ❌ **Making promises you can't keep.** Never say "we'll eliminate all your IT risk." Use the guarantee language only.
- ❌ **Arguing with objections.** Never argue. Validate, then redirect: *"I get that — and that makes sense. Can I ask you one quick question about that?"*
- ❌ **Giving up after the first "no."** Most bookings happen after the second, third, or fourth "no" — the first is almost never the real answer. One soft redirect is always appropriate.
- ❌ **Not doing the end-of-day report.** You can't improve what you don't track. Miss it and you're flying blind.
- ❌ **Logging calls vaguely.** *Bad:* "No answer x5." *Good:* "Called Wed/Thur/Fri, left voicemail each time. Texted Thursday — no reply. Office manager's name is Sarah, medical practice in [town]. Try again Monday 8:30 AM."

## 11. FAQ for New Callers

**Q: What if I don't know the answer to something they ask about the service?**
A: *"That's a great question — and honestly, that's exactly why we do the 15-minute chat first. The call is so we can go through the specifics properly rather than guessing over the phone. Can we get you booked in?"* Never bluff. Never pretend. Redirect to the booking.

**Q: What if they ask how I got their number?**
A: Be honest — a business database or LinkedIn. Dodging the question makes it worse.

**Q: How many times should I try a number before giving up?**
A: 7-8 attempts over 7 days across different times of day. After that, mark as dead and move on.

**Q: What if someone is really rude or aggressive?**
A: Stay calm. *"No worries at all, I appreciate your time."* Hang up. Do not engage with aggression, ever. Log it and move on.

**Q: What if they book a call and then no-show?**
A: Text within 10 minutes: *"Hey [Name] — looks like we missed each other. Happy to reschedule if this week is still relevant for you? Just let me know."* One follow-up only. If no reply, mark as dead.

**Q: What's the best time of day to call?**
A: 8-9 AM, 11:30 AM-12:30 PM, 4-5:30 PM. Avoid Monday mornings and Friday afternoons. Track what works best for your specific list — it varies by vertical (medical vs. AEC).

**Q: Can I adjust the script?**
A: The structure is fixed. The exact wording can flex once you've done 500+ calls and know the framework cold. Until then, stick to it exactly.
