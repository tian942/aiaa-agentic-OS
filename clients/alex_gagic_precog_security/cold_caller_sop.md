## 1. What You're Selling

**Company:** PreCog Security

**What we do / How does it work:** PreCog Security runs full, manual penetration tests — not the automated vulnerability scans a good chunk of the industry mislabels as pen tests. Engineers spend two to three weeks actually inside a client's systems: scanning, validating, manually escalating vulnerabilities to see how far an attacker could really get, then reporting it in plain terms. Every test ends with a dedicated report review call — not a PDF dropped in an inbox and forgotten.

Target buyer: growing SMBs ($750K+ yearly revenue, 30-100 employees) in regulated or reputation-sensitive industries — fintech, healthtech, legal, insurance — who are either chasing their first big enterprise client (who'll demand a security questionnaire and a compliance certificate before signing) or trying to pass an audit (SOC 2, ISO 27001, HIPAA, GDPR) without an in-house security team.

### Pricing

**No fixed public price point.** PreCog quotes a custom scope of work per engagement based on the client's actual systems and compliance needs — there is no tiered package to recite on a call.

- **What to say instead:** frame value, not a number. Average client lifetime value is $100K-$150K over multi-year relationships (PreCog's longest client, Array Technologies, has been with them 4+ years).
- **Qualification signal:** $750K+ yearly revenue implies roughly a $20-30K+ yearly security budget — that's the rough floor for a real conversation.
- **Guarantee: there isn't one, and don't invent one.** Alex was direct about this on his own onboarding call: *"the cybersecurity industry as a whole haven't come up to that problem yet."* The only thing floated was an unconfirmed idea — money back if a client gets hacked after following PreCog's remediation guidance — and it was explicitly "an idea, I need to think about it," not a committed offer. **Do not promise this on a call.** Lean on the track record instead (7 years, real client tenure, real proof) the same way Tian coached Alex directly: *"Apple doesn't need a guarantee... you have plenty of testimonials."*

### PreCog Security Deliverable Stack

- **Full Penetration Testing:** manual, 2-3 week engagement — scanning, vulnerability validation, manual escalation — not a scan-and-report shortcut
- **Report Review Calls:** a dedicated call per test to walk through findings, not a report dropped with no follow-up (a direct differentiator from most competitors, including the big consulting firms)
- **Remediation Guidance (launching):** step-by-step fix instructions plus engineer consulting time — most of the industry finds vulnerabilities but nobody helps fix them; this is PreCog's next big differentiator
- **Compliance Support:** SOC 2, ISO 27001, HIPAA, GDPR readiness — including emergency-timeline certification support (see the micro-story below)
- **Virtual CISO / "Security Department":** long-tenure clients get PreCog as their ongoing, informal security department — advisory, planning, repeat testing

## 2. Your Mindset

Cold calling is not about selling. It's about uncovering pain and getting agreement to have a deeper conversation.

You are not trying to close someone on the phone. You are trying to book a 15-minute chat. That's it. Your job ends when they say yes to a meeting.

**The Rules of a Great Cold Caller:**

1. **You are curious, not pushy.** You genuinely want to understand their business. When you're curious, they open up. When you're pushy, they hang up.
2. **Rejection is irrelevant.** Every "no" you hear is just math. If 1 in every 100 calls books, then each "no" is worth something — it gets you closer to the "yes." Don't react emotionally to rejection. It means nothing.
3. **You represent a real solution.** The businesses we call have real, often invisible security gaps. We actually find them and help fix them. You're not a pest. You're someone with an answer to a problem they've already been quietly worried about.
4. **You don't give a fuck.** They don't know who you are, they don't care what you do and why you are there. If you freeze — fuck it and move on.

## 4. Objection Handling Library

**Early Objections (Before You Even Get Started)**

- **"I'm not interested."** → *"Of course you're not interested. Most people in your position have never had a real pen test done — just a vulnerability scan somebody sold them as one. What we do is different."* (If they engage even slightly, you're back in.)
- **"Send me some information."** → *"Happy to, but when was sending some information better than a genuine 3 minute talk??"*
- **"We've already got someone for that."** → *"That's actually perfect, so you're already thinking about it. I'm not here to replace anyone. The question I'd be curious about is, are you confident that what you have today is a real pen test, not just a vulnerability scan wearing that label?"*
- **"I don't have time right now."** → *"Completely get it. The call itself is only 15 minutes, just let me know which day and time works best for you and I'll get you booked in?"*
- **"How did you get my number?"** → *"Honestly, I just have a list of the most amazing growing companies in fintech, healthtech, legal, and insurance. Happy to explain more, but first, it seems like you're pretty busy already right?"*

**Mid-Call Objections**

- **"We're not doing anything with security right now."** → *"That's exactly what most people tell us — right up until an enterprise client sends them a 700-question security due-diligence form with a 10-day deadline. Is that something you'd rather be ready for before it happens, or after?"*
- **"Your pricing is higher than other options I've seen."** → *"Yeah, and the ones that are cheaper are almost always a vulnerability scan being sold as a pen test — the two aren't the same thing, and it's a real problem in this industry. A scan tells you what might be wrong. We manually validate and escalate every finding to see how far an attacker could actually get."*
- **"I need to think about it."** → *"Of course, what specifically do you need to think through? Is it timing, budget, or something about the process?"*
- **"We don't have the budget right now."** → *"Totally understand. Can I ask, when you say budget, is it a cash flow thing right now, or more that you're not sure it's worth the spend yet?"*

## 9. Tone & Delivery Rules

Script words matter 20%. Tone matters 80%.

1. **Sound confused, not sharp.** Sounding sharp and professional immediately signals "salesperson" — their guard goes up. Sound slightly unsure, like you're genuinely working something out. *Wrong:* "Hi this is James calling from PreCog Security with a solution for your compliance challenges—" *Right:* "Hi, is this [Name]? Hey — would I completely destroy your day if I told you this is a cold call?"
2. **Slow down after the opener.** Most callers rush, scared of silence. Silence is your friend — it creates space for the prospect to respond. Ask, then stop talking. Wait.
3. **Drop your energy slightly when they push back.** Don't match resistance with energy. When they say "I'm not interested," don't perk up and pitch harder — lower your energy, slow your speech, sound like you genuinely accept what they said, then ask the one question that might open them back up.
4. **Smile when you deliver a light, playful line.** You can hear a smile on the phone. Playful, light — not defensive or salesy.
5. **Mirror their energy in the discovery phase.** Detailed and analytical → match precision. Casual and chatty → be more relaxed. Busy and direct → get to the point faster.
6. **Never sound like you need this call.** As soon as desperation enters your voice, the call dies. You are one of many options; they are one of many leads. No attachment to the outcome.

## 10. Common Mistakes to Never Make

- ❌ **Pitching before you've found pain.** The only time you pitch is after they've told you their pain, you've reflected it back, and they've agreed. Before that — questions only.
- ❌ **Talking too much.** A call where they spoke 70% of the time is a good call. A call where you spoke 70% of the time is a bad call.
- ❌ **Making promises you can't keep — especially a guarantee.** PreCog does not have a committed guarantee. Never invent one on a call. Use the track record and testimonials instead.
- ❌ **Arguing with objections.** Never argue. Validate, then redirect: *"I get that — and that makes sense. Can I ask you one quick question about that?"*
- ❌ **Giving up after the first "no."** Most bookings happen after the second, third, or fourth "no" — the first is almost never the real answer. One soft redirect is always appropriate.
- ❌ **Not doing the end-of-day report.** You can't improve what you don't track. Miss it and you're flying blind.
- ❌ **Logging calls vaguely.** *Bad:* "No answer x5." *Good:* "Called Wed/Thur/Fri, left voicemail each time. Texted Thursday — no reply. CTO's name is Priya, fintech company in Austin. Try again Monday 8:30 AM."

## 11. FAQ for New Callers

**Q: What if I don't know the answer to something they ask about the service?**
A: *"That's a great question — and honestly, that's exactly why we do the 15-minute chat first. The call is so we can go through the specifics properly rather than guessing over the phone. Can we get you booked in?"* Never bluff. Never pretend. Redirect to the booking.

**Q: What if they ask how I got their number?**
A: Be honest — a business database or LinkedIn. Dodging the question makes it worse.

**Q: How many times should I try a number before giving up?**
A: 7-8 attempts over 7 days across different times of day. After that, mark as dead and move on.

**Q: What if someone is really rude or aggressive?**
A: Stay calm. *"No worries at all, I appreciate your time."* Hang up. Do not engage with aggression, ever. Log it and move on.

**Q: What if they book a call and then no-show?**
A: Text within 10 minutes: *"Hey [Name] — looks like we missed each other. Happy to reschedule if this week is still relevant for you? Just let me know."* One follow-up only. If no reply, mark as dead.

**Q: What's the best time of day to call?**
A: 8-9 AM, 11:30 AM-12:30 PM, 4-5:30 PM. Avoid Monday mornings and Friday afternoons. Track what works best for your specific list.

**Q: Can I adjust the script?**
A: The structure is fixed. The exact wording can flex once you've done 500+ calls and know the framework cold. Until then, stick to it exactly.

**Q: A prospect asks if PreCog offers a guarantee. What do I say?**
A: *"We don't do guarantees — this isn't that kind of industry, and honestly, be careful of anyone who does. What we've got instead is seven years and real long-term clients who stay for years, not one-off projects."* Never improvise a guarantee that isn't in Section 1.
