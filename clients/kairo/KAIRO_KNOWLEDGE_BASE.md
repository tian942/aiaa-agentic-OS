## PART 2 — What Kairo Actually Does: Two Business Lines

Kairo runs two distinct things that both use cold calling, and callers/candidates sometimes conflate them. Keep them separate in your head:

**2A. Kairo sells its own growth/marketing services** — Kairo is the agency, a business owner is the client, and Kairo runs their marketing (cold email, ads, funnels, cold calling, etc.) for them.

**2B. Kairo sells cold calling as a done-for-you service** — Kairo builds and runs an entire cold-calling operation *on behalf of* another business (PreCog, ABCNS, Alvearium Cyber, etc.), calling that business's prospects to book appointments on that business's calendar. This is what most of Kairo's cold callers are actually doing day to day — dialing for a client account, not for Kairo itself. **Kairo *is* "the cold calling agency."**

A caller might be booking calls for Kairo's own offer (2A) *or* for one of Kairo's client accounts (2B) — always check which account they're assigned to before answering an offer/pricing question.

### 2B — Kairo's Cold Calling Service (what Kairo sells to other businesses)

Kairo builds and runs a full cold-calling operation for a client: infrastructure, script, list, hiring/training/managing dedicated callers, daily QA, CRM, tracking, and reporting — all the way down to appointments landing directly on the client's calendar.

**Qualified appointment definition — same bar everywhere, every account:** ICP match + showed up to the call.

**The 4-role delivery system:**
| Role | Who (today) | Owns |
|---|---|---|
| Fulfillment Lead | Tian | Onboarding call, ICP/offer/list criteria, script approval, client relationship |
| List Builder | — | Apify scraping + AI Ark phone enrichment, dedupe, loads tracker |
| Cold Caller | — | Dials assigned list, follows script, books + confirms appointments, logs everything, submits EOD |
| Calling Manager / QA Lead | Ben (or whoever owns the floor once 2–3+ callers are live) | Hires/certifies/trains callers, weekly call recording review, KPI tracking, fire/hire calls, weekly client report |

**Full process map:**
```
Client signs → Onboarding call → List building (Apify + AI Ark)
    → Script development → Caller assignment (hire + train if new)
    → Warmup ramp (new numbers/callers) → Live dialing
    → Booked appointments → Show-up management → Client's calendar
    → Weekly QA review + weekly client report
    → Performance review (keep/coach/replace) + list refresh
    → Continuous improvement (script/list/caller get sharper every week)
```
Onboarding happens once. Everything after that repeats weekly for the life of the contract.

**Tools used across the floor:** QUO (live dialer), CloudTalk (used in candidate onboarding/number provisioning), Apify (list scraping), AI Ark (phone enrichment/verification), Tally (EOD report form), Google Sheets (per-client trackers).

**Script skeleton every client script is built from (the "Quick to Cash" framework):**
```
1. OPENER — pattern interrupt + permission ask
2. DESIRED OUTCOME — what do they want more of, get specific, get a number
3. THE GAP — what's stopping them right now
4. THE MECHANISM — reframe the real problem (not what they think it is)
5. MICRO STORY — 1-2 short, specific, real proof stories
6. INTRODUCE THE OFFER — framed around the mechanism, not a feature list
7. THE FREE VALUE STEP — the low-friction next step (audit/plan/diagnostic call)
8. TRIAL CLOSE — summarize what they've agreed to
9. BOOK THE MEETING — never "would you like to book a call," offer a choice instead
```
Rule that never changes across clients: **the caller does not pitch until the prospect has told them their pain and agreed it's real.**

### The 30-Day Cold Calling Client Launch (why a brand-new account has no proof yet)

Every new cold-calling client goes through the same fixed 30-day build. This is the direct explanation for the OTE scenario Tian ran into — **a caller on a brand-new account has no booked-call history to point to yet, and that's expected, not a red flag:**

## PART 3 — The Cold-Calling Floor: Ops, KPIs, Onboarding

**The Setter/Sales Manager is accountable for exactly two numbers — nothing else:**
| KPI | Target |
|---|---|
| Caller Activation Speed | Under 70 hours from first WhatsApp reach-out to a caller live and dialing (2 calls minimum inside that window) |
| Revenue per Caller | Minimum $750/week per caller |

**Onboarding pipeline — candidate to live caller (steady-state hiring, 5 steps):**
1. **Hiring** — all applicants tracked in the Hiring Sheet, status flow `REACHED OUT → RAMP UP → ACTIVE` (or `Left`)
2. **WhatsApp reach-out, in order:** (1) intro message asking for a call same day — *this starts the 70-hour clock* — (2) onboarding materials (welcome email + 2 short videos) once the intro call happens — (3) CloudTalk invite + 4-item pre-dial checklist once materials are confirmed done
3. **Interview format:** one-on-one WhatsApp calls, same day if possible — no group interviews for steady-state hiring (good candidates move fast). *Note: the formal 30-Day Client Launch process (used only when standing up a brand-new client account from scratch) does use a group interview on Day 8 — that's a different, heavier process for a first-time buildout, not the day-to-day hiring loop.*
4. **Onboarding materials** confirmed complete before the CloudTalk invite goes out
5. **Assign, warm up, go live** — assigned to a client, added to that client's tracker, warmup ramp begins

**Warmup ramp (new number or new caller — applies everywhere):**
| Day | Max Dials |
|---|---|
| 1 | 30 |
| 2 | 45 |
| 3 | 60 |
| 4 | 60 |
| 5–7 | 50 |
| 8+ | +7/day, no hard cap |

**Pre-dial checklist — all 5 required before a caller starts:** access to lead list, access to tracker, knows how to file EOD by email, knows how to file EOD on the sheet, understands the warmup process.

**Caller KPI benchmarks — same table, every client account:**
| Metric | Minimum | Target | Great |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dials/day | 100 | 150 | 200+ |
| Contact rate | 10% | 20% | 30%+ |
| Contacts/day | 10 | 20–30 | 40+ |
| Booking rate (of contacts) | 5% | 10% | 15%+ |
| Booked calls/day | 1 | 2 | 3+ |
| Show rate of booked calls | — | 70% | 85%+ |

**Fire/hire policy:** first miss = flagged, same-day conversation. Three misses in a week = formal warning. Repeated pattern after a warning = off the team, no exceptions. This team runs on speed in both directions — hire fast, fire fast.

**Daily manager loop:** check every caller's tracker against ramp/minimums → chase anyone under-dialing or missing days the same day → run the daily Team Huddle (10–15 min, 10:00 AM Eastern) → check EOD reports daily, follow up same day on anything missing.

## PART 6 — FAQ: What Callers and Candidates Actually Ask

*(Answers below are grounded in the sections above — same citation discipline. Where the honest answer is "I don't know yet," that's the answer.)*

**Q: What's the OTE / how much can I realistically make?**
A: Depends entirely on which account you're on — see the pay table in Part 4. If your account is brand new, say so honestly and point to real numbers from other Kairo accounts rather than guessing. Never invent a figure.

**Q: Is this a base salary or fully commission?**
A: It's structured per account (Part 4) — e.g. the Kairo account has a $300 base that activates after 10 booked appointments, plus 5% commission on closed deals; PreCog and ABCNS pay a flat $30 per qualified shown call plus 5% commission. It is not a traditional flat salary on any current account.

**Q: When and how do I get paid?**
A: ⚠️ Not confirmed in current docs for the live offer stack — don't quote a timeline. Say you'll confirm and follow up.

**Q: What's Kairo? Is this a real company?**
A: Kairo Enterprises (formerly Win Marketing) — a growth firm, tagline "Weird Is Normal." It runs its own client acquisition (Part 2A) and also sells done-for-you cold calling as a service to other businesses like PreCog, ABCNS, and Alvearium Cyber (Part 2B). Real, documented case studies: RicFit $40K/mo → $150K/mo, Parsity tripled sales, a $50K deal closed with Alvearium Cyber.

**Q: Who exactly am I calling for, and what am I offering them?**
A: Depends on your assigned account — check the Client Directory (Part 3) and the matching client card (Part 5) before your first dial. Every account has its own script and its own approved offer language; never mix scripts between accounts.

**Q: What happens after I book a call — do I still get credit if it no-shows or doesn't close?**
A: A booked call only counts as "qualified" once it shows (ICP match + showed up — the standard bar everywhere). Commission is earned on **closed** deals specifically, on top of the base/show fee. Show-rate management (reminder texts, day-before and 1-hour-before) is the caller's job, not just the client's calendar system — a no-show is a wasted appointment either way, so the reminder sequence in Part 2B matters directly to your pay.

**Q: What tools do I need?**
A: CloudTalk (you'll be invited to this as part of onboarding — confirm access before your first call) and QUO (the live dialer). Lists live in Apify/AI Ark-enriched trackers you don't need direct access to, just your assigned tab.

**Q: What's the schedule / do I get days off?**
A: Default is 1–2 days off per week (typically Saturday/Sunday on the Kairo account). Power hours to prioritize: 8–9 AM, 11:30 AM–12:30 PM, 4–5:30 PM in the prospect's timezone. 2x/week sales training is mandatory, not optional, on every account.

**Q: What if I miss my dial/booking targets?**
A: First miss = flagged, same-day conversation. Three misses in a week = formal warning. A repeated pattern after that = off the team. This is enforced the same way on every account (Part 3).

**Q: Can the client I'm calling for offer a guarantee if a prospect asks?**
A: Only if it's explicitly documented for that account. PreCog and Alvearium Cyber both explicitly have **no guarantee** — never invent one for either, even under pressure; lean on track record and tenure instead (their client cards in Part 5 have the exact lines to use). Kairo's own offer does have a documented guarantee (Part 2A) — but only for the Main tier, with stated qualification requirements.

**Q: Can I adjust the script if I think I've got a better way to say it?**
A: Structure is fixed across every account. Wording can flex once you've done 500+ calls and know the framework cold — until then, stick to it exactly.

**Q: How many times do I try a number before giving up?**
A: 7–8 attempts across different times of day, over 7 days. After that, mark dead and move on.

**Q: I don't know the answer to something a prospect asked about the client's service — what do I say?**
A: Never bluff, never guess. Redirect to the booking: *"That's a great question — and honestly, that's exactly why we do the [15-minute chat / health check / whatever the client's next step is called] — so we can go through the specifics properly instead of me guessing over the phone. Can we get you booked in?"*

**Q: What's the difference between calling for Kairo's own offer vs. calling for a client like PreCog or ABCNS?**
A: Same mindset, same daily standards, same KPI bar, same warmup ramp — every account runs on the identical operating system (Part 2B, Part 3). What changes per account is Section 1 (the offer/pricing/proof you're pitching) and the objection library language. Never assume Kairo's own pay, guarantee, or pricing rules apply to a client account, or vice versa — always check the specific account's card (Part 4, Part 5).
