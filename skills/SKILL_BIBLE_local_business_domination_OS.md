# MODULE 1: THE STRATEGIC FRAMEWORK
### Priority: 🔴 CRITICAL | Applies to: Every Client

## What It Is
Alex Hormozi's framework from *$100M Leads* states that every business gets customers through exactly 4 channels. The game is finding your unfair advantage in 1-2 of them and over-investing there.

## The Core Four — Applied to Home Service

| Channel | Home Service Translation | Priority |
|---------|--------------------------|----------|
| Post Free Content | Local SEO, GBP, Maps, AI Search | HIGH |
| Run Paid Ads | Google LSA, PPC, Meta | HIGH |
| Warm Outreach | Database reactivation, referrals, B2B partnerships | MEDIUM |
| Cold Outreach | Doornocking, cold calling | LOW (except roofing/pest) |

**Why only two channels matter for home service:** Home service purchases are need-driven and time-sensitive. A homeowner whose AC breaks in July is not on Instagram — they're on Google with a credit card. That intent-driven search behavior makes Local SEO and Google Ads the only two channels where 95% of marketing leverage lives.

**Strategic rule for Kairo:** Never let clients spread budget equally across all four. Concentrate on the two that match buying intent. Build that machine. Layer warm outreach and partnerships on top once the core engine runs.

## Two Core Principles (Hormozi)

**Principle 1: Fix the offer before you scale the leads.**
For home service, the offer IS the website experience. A broken website is a lead-destruction machine — every dollar spent on traffic is multiplied by your conversion rate. Fix the converter first.

**Principle 2: Optimize for LTV:CAC, not cost per lead.**
CPL is a vanity metric. A $600 CPL for a $15,000 system replacement is a 25:1 LTV:CAC — printing money. The same $600 CPL for a $400 service call loses $200 per customer. Same number, completely different business. Always think LTV:CAC by service tier.

# MODULE 2: THE WEBSITE / OFFER SYSTEM
### Priority: 🔴 CRITICAL | Do Before Anything Else

## The Problem
99.2% of home service websites convert below 1%. Getting from 1% to 5% = 3-5x more leads from the same traffic, same ad spend.

| Conversion Rate | 1,000 visitors | Monthly leads | Revenue (at $200/lead, 30% close) |
|----------------|---------------|--------------|-----------------------------------|
| 1% (industry average) | 1,000 | 10 | $600/mo |
| 3% (good) | 1,000 | 30 | $1,800/mo |
| 5% (optimized) | 1,000 | 50 | $3,000/mo |

**Same traffic. Same ad spend. 5x the revenue.**

## The Value Equation
> **Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort and Sacrifice)**

Most operators run only the top half (reviews, guarantees) and ignore the bottom half (friction reduction). The bottom half is where compounding wins live.

## TOP HALF: Drive Value Up

### Dream Outcome — The H1 Headline
The H1 headline is the single most important element on the site. It must answer 4 questions in 3 seconds:
1. Can you solve my specific problem?
2. How fast?
3. Will price surprise me?
4. Can I trust you?

**Wrong (category label):** "HVAC Services in Phoenix"
**Right (outcome statement):** "Cool in 90 Minutes — Same-Day HVAC Repair, Flat-Rate Pricing, Phoenix's Most Reviewed Team"

**H1 Formula by Service Type:**

| Service | H1 Formula |
|---------|-----------|
| HVAC | "[Outcome] in [Time] — [Speed Promise], [Price Promise], [City]'s [Social Proof]" |
| Plumbing | "Licensed Plumber On-Site in 60 Minutes — Flat-Rate Repairs, [City] Since [Year]" |
| Roofing | "Free Roof Inspection Today — Claim-Approved Replacements, 50-Year Warranty, [City]'s #1 Crew" |
| Lawn care | "Lush Lawn in 7 Days — Weekly Maintenance From $X, [City]'s Highest-Rated Crew" |
| Sod install | "New Lawn in One Day — Sod Installation, Prep, and Cleanup. Flat-Rate. Same-Week Install." |
| Pest control | "Bug-Free in 24 Hours — Guaranteed Elimination, [City] Licensed & Insured Since [Year]" |

### Perceived Likelihood (Reassurances)
Elements that prevent self-disqualification. Every visitor has an internal skeptic — these answer it directly.

**Must-haves above the fold:**
- Star rating + review count (not buried — first thing they see)
- Owner name and face (humanizes, builds trust)
- License number + "Licensed & Insured" stated explicitly
- Specific named guarantee: "100% Satisfaction Guarantee — We fix it or your money back, no questions asked"

**Throughout the page:**
- Before/after photo galleries (every section, not just one gallery page)
- Certifications as visual badges (NATE, EPA, BBB, state license)
- Specific testimonials with name, city, service type

## BOTTOM HALF: Drive Friction Down (the compounding wins nobody runs)

### Time Delay Killers

**1. Online booking with real calendar visibility**
If client is on ServiceTitan, Jobber, or Housecall Pro — surface the actual booking calendar on the site. "Next available: Today at 2 PM" beats "we'll call to schedule" in conversion every time.

**2. Same-day / 90-minute dispatch promise**
State this prominently. It eliminates "I'll keep looking to see if someone can come faster."

**3. Emergency and after-hours visibility**
If you offer it, the homepage must say so. Put it in the nav and in the hero.

**4. Instant estimate calculator**
Even a rough range ("AC repair: $150-$450") removes the "what is this going to cost" anxiety that kills conversions.

### Effort and Sacrifice Killers

**1. Sticky mobile header (highest-priority CTA on mobile)**
On mobile (65-80% of home service searches), a persistent header with tap-to-call, tap-to-book, tap-to-chat that scrolls with the user. Every scroll without a CTA is a lost lead.

**2. Pricing transparency page**
Not exact prices — ranges. "AC repair: $150-$450 depending on the part. Freon recharge: $250-$450." Disarms the "I don't know what I'm getting into" objection.

**3. Address autocomplete on forms**
Fewer keystrokes = more completions.

**4. Photo upload from phone**
For quote-required jobs (roof damage, sod area, water damage), let them photograph and submit from the form page. You can quote without a site visit. Competitor who requires a physical visit loses.

**5. Financing badge**
"Financing available — as low as $X/month" reduces sticker shock on big-ticket work.

## CRO Tools
- **Hotjar / Microsoft Clarity** (free) — heatmaps to see where users drop off
- **Google Optimize / VWO** — A/B testing headlines, CTAs
- **CallRail** — track which page visitors call (see Module 13)

- [ ] Certifications displayed as visual badges

# MODULE 3: LOCAL SEO — MAP PACK SYSTEM
### Priority: 🔴 CRITICAL | Primary Source of Free Leads

## The Three Google Surfaces
Every home service business needs to appear in all three:
1. **Map Pack (3-Pack)** — the map listing section
2. **AI Mode / ChatGPT** — covered in Module 5
3. **Organic / SERP** — covered in Module 6

When a client shows up in all three, the customer sees them **three times before seeing a competitor once.**

## THE NAME MATCH CHEAT CODE
**This is the #1 ranking factor most people never implement.**

Google weights the business name as a keyword signal for map pack relevance. "Smith HVAC" outranks "Smith Brothers" for HVAC searches even with inferior reviews and backlinks — simply because the name contains the keyword.

### The DBA Strategy (Step-by-Step)
1. Client files a DBA ("Doing Business As") with their county/state — $50-$150, typically 1 day
2. DBA name = `[Primary Keyword] + [City]` or `[City] + [Primary Keyword]`
   - Good: "Phoenix HVAC Pros", "Manitowoc Sod Installation", "Dallas Plumbing Experts"
3. Update Google Business Profile name to the DBA name
4. Update ALL citations to match exactly

**Warning:** Do NOT keyword stuff — "Best HVAC Air Conditioning Repair Phoenix Emergency Service" = suspension. One keyword addition to a legitimate name is defensible. Multiple keywords is a ban.

**Pitch hack:** Before any client pitch, search their primary keyword in their city. If the #1 map pack result has the keyword in its business name — show them on screen. They've never seen this before. Closes deals.

## Full GBP Optimization Checklist

### Category Selection (Most Critical Mistake)
Most operators pick the wrong primary category and wonder why they don't rank for high-intent searches.

| If you want to rank for... | Primary Category | Secondary |
|---------------------------|-----------------|-----------|
| AC repair searches | "Air Conditioning Repair Service" | "HVAC Contractor" |
| Emergency plumber | "Plumber" or "Emergency Plumbing Service" | others |
| Lawn maintenance | "Lawn Care Service" | "Landscaping" |
| Sod installation | "Sod Supplier" | "Lawn Care Service", "Landscaping" |
| Roof repair | "Roofing Contractor" | "Roof Repair Service" |
| Pest control | "Pest Control Service" | "Exterminator" |

List ALL relevant secondary categories — every additional relevant category is an additional keyword signal.

### Service Descriptions (Free Keyword Insertion)
Every service gets a 2-3 sentence keyword-rich description. Most operators leave this blank.

**Template:**
> "We provide [service] services throughout [city] and [surrounding areas]. Our [credential] team [specific capability], from [problem A] to [problem B]. [Price positioning or speed promise]."

**Example (AC Repair):**
> "We provide same-day air conditioning repair services throughout Phoenix and the East Valley. Our NATE-certified technicians diagnose all makes and models, from refrigerant leaks to compressor failures. Flat-rate pricing — no surprise fees."

### Products Tab (Hidden AI Ranking Signal)
Add your services as "Products" in GBP (in addition to Services). This creates structured data that ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, and Claude read when building AI recommendations. Almost no operator does this. Takes 20 minutes, pays off for years.

### Geotagged Photos — Weekly Upload System
Google factors profile activity and photo freshness. A profile that uploads 50 photos at setup and stops is treated differently than one uploading 5-10 photos per week consistently.

**Field-level system:**
1. Every tech photographs every completed job before leaving (exterior shot, work area, before/after if possible)
2. Photos uploaded to shared Google Drive folder (named by date + job type)
3. Weekly: admin downloads, geotags photos using phone native location or GeoImgr, uploads to GBP
4. Geotag embed: EXIF GPS data signals service area breadth to the algorithm

### GBP Posts — Weekly (Overlooked Internal Linking Tool)
Post weekly with "Learn More" button linking to specific service pages (not the homepage). Creates internal link signals from GBP to service pages, keeping them indexed.

**Weekly post calendar template:**
- Monday: Seasonal service offer → links to that service page
- Wednesday: Educational tip + service mention → links to service page
- Friday: Urgency/availability post → links to booking page or contact

- [ ] All relevant secondary categories added
- [ ] DBA name match (or plan to implement)
- [ ] Keyword-rich service description for every service
- [ ] Services also added as Products
- [ ] 50+ photos uploaded (geotagged)

# MODULE 4: REVIEW VELOCITY SYSTEM
### Priority: 🔴 CRITICAL | Fuel for Everything Else

## Why This Is Non-Negotiable
- Review velocity (consistency month-over-month) matters more than total count
- A profile going from 50 → 200 reviews in 3 months signals organic growth to Google
- 500 reviews with zero new in 6 months = stagnating in the algorithm
- Target for competitive markets (HVAC, plumbing, electrical, roofing): **30+ reviews/month**
- Multi-platform reviews create consensus signals for AI search (ChatGPT, Gemini read Yelp, Trustpilot, Google separately)

## The Complete Field Protocol

### Step 1: Tech completes job, confirms customer is happy
**Only ask happy customers.** An unhappy customer asked for a review = guaranteed 1-star.

### Step 2: In-person ask BEFORE leaving (most important step)
Most companies skip to text/email. Don't. The in-person ask converts dramatically higher because of reciprocity and social presence. The customer just benefited from your service. The tech is physically there. The social pressure to say yes is real and positive.

### Step 3: Exact Script — Do Not Improvise
> *"Hey [name], if you were happy with the work today, it would mean a lot to me personally if you left us a review. I actually get a small bonus when customers do, and it literally takes about 20 seconds. Would you mind just scanning this real quick?"*

**Why this language works:**
- "Means a lot to me personally" → liking + reciprocity
- "I get a small bonus" → honest, gives them a reason to help
- "20 seconds" → removes perceived effort barrier

### Step 4: QR Code Card
Laminated card, lives in every tech's work bag permanently. Links directly to the Google review write page (deep link that opens the review box immediately — not just the profile page).

**How to get the deep link:** Go to your Google Business Profile → Get More Reviews → copy the link. Shorten with bit.ly. Encode in QR. Print laminated cards.

### Step 5: Incentivize the Tech
- Standard markets: $25-$50 per review
- Competitive markets (HVAC Phoenix/Miami/Dallas): up to $300 per review
- This is employee compensation for a sales behavior — legal and ethical
- Track with a simple spreadsheet: Tech name + job address + review date = bonus paid

### Step 6: 3-Touch Follow-Up If No In-Person Review
| Timing | Channel | Message |
|--------|---------|---------|
| 12 hours post-job | SMS | "Hi [name], this is [company]. We finished your [service] today — a quick Google review would mean a lot to our team: [direct link]" |
| 24 hours | Email | Same ask, slightly expanded — mention it helps small local businesses |
| 48 hours | Phone call | Owner or manager calls, positioned as quality check. Natural review ask at end: "By the way, if you have a moment, a review would really help us..." |

**The 3-touch system captures 20-30% of people who intended to leave a review but got distracted.** Most companies stop at the in-person ask or first text.

## Multi-Platform Rotation Schedule
Rotate which platform you prioritize each quarter. Each platform = a separate data point for AI search.

| Quarter | Primary Platform | Secondary |
|---------|-----------------|-----------|
| Q1 | Google | — |
| Q2 | Yelp | Google |
| Q3 | Trustpilot | Google |
| Q4 | Google | Angie's |

# MODULE 5: AI SEARCH SYSTEM
### Priority: 🟡 MEDIUM (Months 2-5) | The Emerging Moat

## Why This Matters Now
ChatGPT has 100M+ daily searches (2025). A significant and growing percentage of local service searches begin with "What's the best HVAC company in [city]?" on ChatGPT, Gemini, or Perplexity. The businesses building these signals now own this channel for 3-5 years.

## How AI Search Works (Different from Google)
Traditional SEO: backlinks + on-page + authority.
AI search: aggregates reviews, citations, social mentions, press mentions, and structured Q&A content — synthesizes a recommendation. You need signals across ALL sources.

## Critical Content Insight: What Ranks vs. What Doesn't

**DOES NOT rank in AI mode (fine for traditional SEO, useless for AI recommendation):**
- "Top 10 HVAC Maintenance Tips"
- "How to Change Your Air Filter"
- "What Is SEER Rating?"

**DOES rank in AI mode and drives booked jobs:**
- "How much does AC repair cost in [city]?" (structured Q&A with your business named)
- "Best HVAC company in [city]" (listicle where you're #1)
- Service pages answering "Does [company] do [service] in [city]?"
- Reviews that describe specific outcomes (not just "great service!")

**Q&A Page Format AI Engines Extract From:**
```
Q: How much does AC repair cost in Phoenix?
A: AC repair in Phoenix typically costs $150–$550 depending on the issue.
Common repairs: refrigerant recharge ($250–$450), capacitor ($150–$300),
contactor ($150–$350), compressor ($1,200–$2,800).
Phoenix HVAC Pros offers flat-rate pricing on all repairs — call for a same-day estimate.
```
This format answers what a customer asks ChatGPT, includes city + service + price + brand — all extractable by AI.

## Phase 1: Foundation (Months 1-2)

### Citation Building — 80-120 Directories
**NAP must be IDENTICAL across every citation.** Not "Main St" on one and "Main Street" on another. Inconsistency = suppressed rankings.

**Priority tiers:**
- Tier 1 (do first): Yelp, BBB, Angie's, HomeAdvisor, Yellow Pages, Apple Maps, Bing Places, Nextdoor
- Tier 2: Houzz, Thumbtack, Porch, Networx, Expertise.com
- Tier 3: Chamber directories, state contractor associations, city business directories

**Tool to automate:** BrightLocal ($29/mo), Whitespark, or Yext ($499+/yr for full automation)

### Website Architecture
**One dedicated page per service:**
- `/ac-repair-phoenix`
- `/furnace-replacement-phoenix`
- `/emergency-hvac-repair-phoenix`

Each service page: H1 with exact service + city, 500-800 words, price range, FAQ section (5+ Q&As minimum), internal links, CTA.

**One dedicated location page per city served:**
- `/hvac-scottsdale`
- `/hvac-tempe`

Each location page: services available in that area, localized content, links to all service pages for that area.

**Internal linking mesh:**
```
Homepage → ALL service pages + ALL location pages (via nav + footer)
Every service page → ALL location pages where available
Every location page → ALL service pages available there
```

## Phase 2: Authority Building (Months 2-5)

### Press Releases — One Per Month
Content doesn't need to be dramatic. New hire, milestone, new service area, charity sponsorship. What matters: **business name + city + primary service keyword** in every release, distributed to 100+ outlets.

**Topics that work:**
- "Phoenix HVAC Pros Hires Three NATE-Certified Technicians for Summer Demand"
- "Phoenix HVAC Pros Reaches 500 Five-Star Google Reviews"
- "Phoenix HVAC Pros Expands Service Area to Include Scottsdale"
- "Phoenix HVAC Pros Partners with [Local Charity] on Community HVAC Project"

**Cost:** $100-$400/release (PRWeb, EIN Presswire, BusinessWire)
**Frequency:** Monthly, non-negotiable

### Listicle Articles — "Best Of" Strategy
Publish "Best HVAC Companies in Phoenix, AZ" articles. List yourself #1. Include real competitors at #2-5 (legitimacy — AI won't trust fake-company articles).

**Publish on:** Company blog + Medium + LinkedIn + guest posts on contracting/home improvement sites.

**Why it works:** When ChatGPT gets asked "Who's the best HVAC company in Phoenix?" and three separate articles name your client #1, that consensus drives the AI recommendation.

**Target:** 2-3 listicle articles per service type, across multiple platforms.

### Local Backlinks
| Source | Cost | Value |
|--------|------|-------|
| Chamber of Commerce | $200-500/yr | Member directory link |
| Supplier/vendor partner pages | Free (ask) | Authorized dealer listing |
| Contractor associations (ACCA, PHCC, NRCA) | Varies | Industry authority |
| Nonprofit sponsorships | $200-500 donation | .org site link |
| Houzz professional profile | Free | High-DA directory |

Local relevance > domain authority for local rankings.

## Phase 3: AI-Specific Signals (Months 4-7)

### Social Mentions
One organic mention on a local Facebook group, Reddit neighborhood thread, or Nextdoor = ~10x the weight of a paid mention for AI search.

**Mobilize happy customers:** After each successful job, add to the review-ask sequence: "If you're in any local Facebook groups or the [neighborhood] subreddit, you'd do us a huge favor mentioning us next time someone asks for a recommendation."

**Non-obvious tactic:** Business owners can coordinate genuine customer experiences shared in community groups. A real customer posting "Just had Phoenix HVAC Pros out — same day, flat rate, incredible" in a local Facebook group is legitimate content and a powerful AI signal.

### Brand + City Signal Consolidation
Every piece of content across every platform should contain: **business name + city + service type together.**

- ❌ "Phoenix HVAC Pros offers great service" (missing service keyword)
- ❌ "The best HVAC repair in Phoenix" (missing brand name)
- ✅ "Phoenix HVAC Pros has been providing AC repair and HVAC installation throughout Phoenix and the East Valley since [year]"

This co-occurrence density is what AI search uses to identify "the HVAC company in Phoenix."

# MODULE 7: GOOGLE ADS SYSTEM
### Priority: 🟠 HIGH | The Volume Dial

## The LTV:CAC Framework — The Core Insight

**CPL is a vanity metric.** LTV:CAC is the only acquisition math that matters.

### How to Calculate True LTV by Service Tier

**LTV is NOT just the first job ticket.** Include:
- First job revenue
- Annual repeat purchase rate
- Maintenance plan enrollment + renewal
- Major replacement likelihood (system age-driven for HVAC)
- Referral rate × referred customer LTV

**LTV Calculation by Vertical:**

| Vertical | Service Tier | First Job | True LTV | Max CAC (5:1) | Max CPL (at 30% close) |
|---------|-------------|-----------|----------|---------------|------------------------|
| HVAC | System replacement | $15,000 | $18,000+ | $3,600 | $1,080 |
| HVAC | Service call | $400 | $1,500 | $300 | $90 |
| Plumbing | Repipe | $8,000 | $10,000 | $2,000 | $600 |
| Plumbing | Emergency repair | $350 | $800 | $160 | $48 |
| Roofing | Full replacement | $12,000 | $15,000 | $3,000 | $900 |
| Lawn care | Annual contract | $2,400/yr | $8,000 | $1,600 | $480 |
| Sod install | Full lawn | $4,000 | $10,000 | $2,000 | $600 |

**Exercise for every client before touching ads:**
1. Pull average ticket by service type from CRM
2. Estimate annual repeat purchase rate
3. Estimate referral rate (ask owner: "What % of customers come from referrals?")
4. Add recurring maintenance/plan revenue
5. Set max CAC = LTV ÷ 5 (5:1 minimum; 10:1 = excellent)
6. Work backward: max CAC × close rate = max CPL per tier

## Campaign Structure by Service Tier

**Broken (before):** "All HVAC Services" — one campaign, all leads weighted equally, cheap leads dominate budget.

**Fixed (after):**

### Tier 1 — High LTV (System Installations/Replacements)
- Keywords: "[service] replacement [city]", "new [system] [city]", "[service] installation [city]"
- Bidding: Aggressive — max CPL calibrated to LTV math
- Ad copy: Quality, warranty, financing, brand credibility
- Landing page: Financing options, system comparison, full trust stack
- Budget: **Primary allocation**

### Tier 2 — Service Calls and Repairs
- Keywords: "[service] repair [city]", "[service] repair near me"
- Bidding: Controlled — max CPL = LTV ÷ 5 ÷ close rate
- Ad copy: Speed, same-day, flat-rate
- Budget: Secondary

### Tier 3 — Maintenance Plans
- Keywords: "[service] tune-up [city]", "[service] maintenance [city]"
- Bidding: Moderate — recurring revenue makes LTV high
- Budget: Third allocation

## Paid Ads Priority Order

**1. Google Local Service Ads (LSA) — Max budget here first**
- Pay-per-lead, not per-click
- Google Guaranteed badge = instant trust signal
- Close rate: 30-45% vs. 15-25% for standard PPC
- **LSA ranking factors:** Total GBP reviews, response time, booking rate
- **Critical:** Answer within 5 minutes. Google's algorithm factors response time into lead distribution. Set push notifications on every dispatcher phone.

**2. Google Search Ads (PPC)**
- Most controllable and customizable

Google Ads click
        ↓
Dynamic Number Insertion (CallRail/WhatConverts)
        ↓
Phone call (tracked to keyword, campaign, ad)
        ↓

# MODULE 14: PARTNERSHIP MARKETING
### Priority: 🟡 MEDIUM | Zero CPL Leads

Referred customers:

## The Three Partner Types

### A. Realtor Partnerships
**Why:** Realtors get asked for contractor recommendations constantly — pre-listing (what needs to be fixed to sell), post-inspection (buyer wants repairs done), and for new buyers (who should I use?). One active realtor routing 3 referrals/month is worth $3,000-6,000/month at zero CPL.

**Structure:**

- For HVAC: $300 job → $30 referral fee → $270 net vs. $127 CPL from Google

**How to land them:**
1. Show up at local real estate office with coffee and a simple leave-behind
2. Offer to be the go-to vendor for their buyers — fast service, consistent communication
3. After every completed referral job: send the realtor a brief update + thank you. This keeps the relationship active.

### B. New Home Builder Partnerships
**Why:** New homeowners need everything in year one — lawn care, HVAC maintenance, gutters, landscaping. New construction neighborhoods are geographically concentrated = efficient routing.

**Structure:**
- Builder includes preferred vendor in closing package
- Negotiate to be exclusive or preferred vendor for a subdivision
- Offer a new-homeowner welcome discount (10-15% off first service)

**How to land:** Relationship with builder's project manager or construction coordinator. Bring coffee, show your work, be easy to deal with.

### C. Insurance Adjuster / Restoration Relationships
**Why:** For restoration companies (water damage, fire, mold), insurance adjuster referrals are the single highest-value lead source. Adjusters steer homeowners to trusted vendors.

**Structure:**
- Fastest response time wins the job — be the company that shows up in 60 minutes, not 4 hours
- Use DASH/CoreLogic to give adjusters real-time job updates (they need this for their carriers)
- Document everything and communicate proactively — this is what adjusters care about most
- Meet adjusters at local industry events (IICRC, restoration trade shows)

**Economics:** Water damage restoration averages $3,000-12,000+. One adjuster routing 3-5 jobs/month = $9,000-60,000/mo at near-zero acquisition cost.
